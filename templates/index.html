  1. The question

    Farmers who sow after the first big rain can be caught by a dry break and have to sow again. We ask: given the weather so far, is this date safe for soybean?

  2. The data

    Daily weather for one point near Ahilyanagar (ERA5 reanalysis from Open-Meteo) and monthly ocean indices from NOAA: ONI, SOI and the Indian Ocean Dipole. Years 1995 to 2025, nine decision dates a year from 18 May to 13 July, 279 rows in all.

  3. The label

    A date counts as safe when at least 75 mm of rain has fallen since 1 May, at least 40 mm falls in the next 21 days, and no dry spell of 10 days follows. These are our own rules, based on common soybean advice.

  4. The features

    Rain since 1 May, rain in the last 7 days, rainy days in the last 14, days since the last rain, topsoil moisture, evaporation demand, night temperature, ONI, ONI change and SOI. Only data from before the decision date is used.

  6. What we found

    A one-line rain rule scores about as well as our Random Forest. The model cannot yet tell a false start from a real one, because that depends on rain still to come.

  7. Limits and next steps

    Rainfall is reanalysis at one point, not gauge data. Labels come from our rule, not official sowing dates. In 2025 the rule called late May safe, although farmers were advised to wait. Next: weather forecasts, gauge data from MPKV Rahuri, and more locations.
